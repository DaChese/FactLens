What it does

A second layer for live reporting

01

Recognizes the story

Uses captions, audio transcription, page titles, and visible text to identify the event being discussed.

02

Compares coverage

Finds reporting on the same story and highlights concrete details present elsewhere but absent from the segment.

03

Shows its work

Links source articles, quotes exact transcript evidence, timestamps results, and separates outlet history from segment analysis.

04

Supports follow-up checks

Runs statement checks and public-reaction summaries only when requested, keeping reporting and public discussion distinct.

How it works

From live segment to inspectable note

  1. ListenCaptions or a short audio buffer provide the segment text.
  2. MatchPage signals and current headlines confirm that FactLens found the right story.
  3. CompareOther reporting supplies source-linked context and coverage distribution.
  4. AssessA versioned rubric examines framing while server checks reject malformed or invented evidence.
  5. ReviewThe note appears in the side panel, ready for evidence inspection or optional follow-up checks.

How findings are determined

Signals, safeguards, and visible uncertainty

FactLens does not reduce bias to an outlet label. It evaluates the specific segment and exposes the inputs behind the result.

SignalQuestion
Loaded languageIs wording neutral or emotionally charged?
Source balanceAre relevant perspectives represented?
Evidence qualityAre claims supported and attributed?
Missing contextDoes comparison coverage reveal a material omission?
Fact/opinion separationCan viewers distinguish reporting from interpretation?

Built-in guardrails

  • Low-confidence story matches are withheld.
  • Required scores must be complete and valid.
  • Evidence must occur verbatim in the transcript.
  • Analysis completeness is not presented as accuracy.
  • Political direction remains explicitly experimental.
